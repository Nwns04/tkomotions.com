Most small businesses that ask about automation are already thinking about the wrong problem.

They want to automate **customer service** because that feels expensive. Or **invoicing** because it feels tedious. Or **social media** because someone said AI can do it.

None of those are usually where the real time is lost.

## Start with the workflow, not the tool

Before you pick a tool, write down the actual sequence of what happens when a customer contacts you, places an order, or asks for a quote.

You will usually find three things:

1. **The handoff** — where one person's work becomes another person's problem.
2. **The wait** — where the process stops because someone hasn't responded yet.
3. **The re-entry** — where the same information gets typed into a second system.

Automation that fixes any of those three is worth doing. Automation that just moves the same work to a different screen is not.

## The test

If you can't describe the before and after of a single workflow in two sentences, you don't have a problem worth automating yet. You have a feeling.

Find the workflow. Then automate.
